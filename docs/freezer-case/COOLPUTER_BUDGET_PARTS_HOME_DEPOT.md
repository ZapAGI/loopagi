## Category 1: Copper Tubing & Heat Exchange (Home Depot)

The heart of the cooling system — copper coils submerged in the
reservoir to chill the coolant via the freezer's cold air.

| Item | HD SKU | Size | Price | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Everbilt 3/8" × 20 ft Soft Copper Refrigeration Coil** | D-06020PS / #202287075 | 3/8" OD × 20 ft | ~$35-45 | Main chiller coil — wind into reservoir |
| **Everbilt 1/4" × 20 ft Soft Copper Refrigeration Coil** | D-04020PS / #100343040 | 1/4" OD × 20 ft | ~$20-28 | Secondary coil or sensor routing |
| **Everbilt 3/8" × 10 ft Soft Copper Coil** (if 20 ft is overkill) | D-06010PS / #100343482 | 3/8" OD × 10 ft | ~$18-25 | Budget option — still enough for 8-10 coil wraps |

**DIY Budget Tip:** The 3/8" × 20 ft coil is the sweet spot. Wind it
around a 4-5" diameter form (like a coffee can) to create a helical
evaporator coil. 20 ft gives you ~15 wraps — massive surface area for
heat exchange. One coil is sufficient.

**Improvement: Double-Coil Design**
Buy TWO 10 ft coils instead of one 20 ft — run them in parallel
(Y-split). This doubles flow rate through the cold zone and reduces
pressure drop vs one long 20 ft coil.

**Improvement: Use Compression Fittings Over Barbs**
Compression fittings on copper are leak-proof without clamps. Barb
fittings on soft tubing need hose clamps. For the copper-to-rubber
tubing transitions, use compression + barb adapters.

G1/4" to 3/8" barb adapters — these are PC-specific and must come
from Amazon (~$4 each, buy a 6-pack for ~$18).

**Improvement: Triple-Layer Insulation Strategy**
1. **Layer 1 (structural):** 2" XPS foam board — cut to fit as thermal
   barriers between zones
2. **Layer 2 (sealing):** Great Stuff spray foam — fill ALL gaps between
   XPS panels and freezer walls
3. **Layer 3 (condensation):** Armaflex pipe wrap on ALL cold tubing +
   neoprene tape on cold surfaces near cable pass-throughs

This triple-layer approach eliminates condensation — no cold surface
is exposed to ambient air.

Mount the pump INSIDE the stock pot reservoir. The pump generates ~20W

connection (fewer fittings, fewer leak points).

## Category 7: Radiator / Heat Exchanger (Budget Alternatives)

Instead of an expensive PC radiator, consider these alternatives:

| Option | Source | Price | Cooling Capacity | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Car Heater Core (universal)** | AutoZone / Amazon | ~$15-25 | Excellent (~5-10 kW) | Copper/aluminum, 5/8" inlet, compact (~7"×7") |
| **Budget PC Radiator 240mm** | Amazon (generic) | ~$15-20 | Good (~400W) | Aluminum, G1/4" threads, fits 2× 120mm fans |
| **Budget PC Radiator 360mm** | Amazon (generic) | ~$20-30 | Very good (~600W) | Aluminum, G1/4" threads, fits 3× 120mm fans |
| **Alphacool NexXxoS 360mm** (quality) | Amazon | ~$55-65 | Excellent (~800W) | Copper fins, best budget-premium option |
| **DIY Copper Coil + Fan** | Home Depot | ~$25-35 | Good (~300-500W) | 10 ft 3/8" copper coil + 120mm fan blowing across it |
| **Duda Energy 10-Plate Brazed Plate HX** | Amazon | ~$30-40 | Overkill (~20 kW) | Industrial, compact, incredible efficiency |

**Best Budget Option: Car Heater Core ($15-25)**

A car heater core is literally an automotive heat exchanger designed to
transfer heat from coolant to air. It handles 5-10 kW easily — our PC
only generates 650W. Available at any AutoZone.

**DIY heater core radiator build:**
1. Buy universal heater core from AutoZone (~$18)
2. Get 5/8" to 3/8" barb reducers from Home Depot (~$4 each)
3. Mount 2× 120mm fans on one side with zip ties (~$0 if reusing fans)
4. Total: ~$26 for a radiator that outperforms most PC radiators

**Improvement: Dual Heat Exchange (Copper Coil + Heater Core)**
- **Stage 1:** Copper coil submerged in reservoir (freezer chills it)
- **Stage 2:** Heater core in external rack (fans pre-cool returning coolant)
- Two-stage cooling = the returning coolant is pre-cooled by the heater
  core BEFORE it reaches the freezer's cold zone = less work for the
  compressor = lower power consumption + colder temps

| Item | Source | Price | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Prime Guard RV Antifreeze (propylene glycol)** | HD #314176974 | ~$7/gal | Food-grade PG, non-toxic, -50°F rated |
| **Distilled Water (1 gal)** | Home Depot / grocery | ~$1.50 | ALWAYS use distilled, never tap |
| **Biocide (Silver Kill Coil or Mayhems Biocide)** | Amazon | ~$6-10 | Prevents algae/bacteria growth |
| **Alternative: Pure Propylene Glycol USP** | Amazon (Sanco) | ~$22/gal | Highest purity, mix your own ratio |

**Coolant Recipe:**
- **60% distilled water + 40% propylene glycol** = freezes at -20°C
- Add biocide (silver coil or 2 drops Mayhems per liter)
- Total coolant needed: ~3-5 liters for the loop
- Cost: ~$10-15 using RV antifreeze + distilled water

**Improvement: Use RV Antifreeze Directly**
Prime Guard RV Antifreeze from Home Depot is **already premixed**
propylene glycol at the right concentration for -50°F protection.
Just pour it in — no mixing needed. $7/gallon. One gallon is enough
for most loops.
